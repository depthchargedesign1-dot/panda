How to pack like a professional remover

The packing techniques our crews use every day, so your belongings arrive in one piece.

Most breakages happen because boxes are overfilled, underfilled or badly labelled. Here’s how our crews pack.

Get the right materials

Use double-walled removal cartons rather than supermarket boxes, plenty of packing paper or tissue, bubble wrap for fragile items and good quality tape. Wardrobe boxes let you hang clothes straight in.

Heavy items in small boxes

Books, tins and tools go in small cartons; bedding, cushions and lampshades in large ones. Keep each box to a weight one person can lift comfortably.

Fill every gap

A box that rattles will break. Fill gaps with crumpled paper, tea towels or soft items so nothing can move, and make sure the lid closes flat so boxes stack safely.

Plates on edge, glasses upright

Wrap plates individually and stand them on their edge. Wrap glasses and stand them upright, with paper stuffed inside. Put a layer of crumpled paper at the bottom and top of every fragile box.

Label clearly

Write the destination room and contents on two sides, and add “FRAGILE” and “THIS WAY UP” where needed. A colour sticker per room makes unloading quicker.

Pack room by room

Finish one room before starting the next, and start with the rooms you use least. Leave the kitchen and bathroom until last.

Keep the essentials separate

Pack a first-night box and a personal bag with documents, medication, chargers, keys and valuables, and take them with you in the car.
